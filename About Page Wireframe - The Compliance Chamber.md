# **About Page Wireframe \- The Compliance Chamber** 

## 1\) Opening Problem Statement

**Purpose**  
Start with the regulatory gap itself. The page should immediately frame the problem the practice exists to solve.

**Designer notes**  
This should feel like a manifesto opener. Big typography, lots of space, maybe one highlighted sentence as the visual anchor. No “meet the founder” layout yet. The page should begin with conviction.

## 2\) The Gap Map / Conflict Matrix

This is most creative section opportunity.

Creative angle:  
 Show the actual tension points the practice resolves:

* AML vs privacy timing conflicts  
* AI explainability vs automation  
* escalation workflows vs reporting duties  
* governance oversight vs operational execution

This can be presented as a matrix, a split-screen conflict map, or stacked cards.

Designer note:  
Could be the signature visual on the About page. Quiet, sharp, user-ready, premium.

## 3\) Founder Lens / How Purva Sees the Problem

This is where the page becomes personal without becoming biographical.

Creative angle:  
 Frame Purva as a regulatory architect.  
 The language can feel like:  
 “I look at regulation the way an architect looks at structure for load-bearing points and blind spots.”

Why it works:  
The questionnaire already gives that “architect / systems / defensibility” language, and the copy brief says the About page should introduce the founder in first person, briefly and precisely.

Designer note:  
This section can carry a portrait or a subtle founder quote, nothing more.

## 4\) The Triangulated Model

This is a brilliant creative section for the About page.

Creative angle:  
 Make the practice model visual.  
 Three pillars:

* Legal defensibility  
* Strategic governance  
* Systems integration

Then show how they operate together.

Why it works:  
Purva ma’am literally uses the idea of triangulation, and the brief asks for the practice to feel like a bridge between legal logic, management logic, and systems logic.

Designer note:  
 This can be a triangle diagram, layered columns, or a three-part editorial block.

6\) How We Work

This can be made creative if it is treated like an operating model instead of a service list.

Creative angle:  
 Show a flow like:  
 observe → map → test → operationalize → defend

Designer note:  
 A vertical process strip or a stepped timeline would work well.
